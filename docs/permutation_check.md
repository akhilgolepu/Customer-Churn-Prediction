# Permutation check: why the degraded candidate's AUC is not 0.5

Generated by `python model/training/permutation_check.py`. Do not edit by hand.

The production recipe was trained on 20 independent permutations of the churn labels (train and validation permuted; early stopping on the permuted validation labels) and scored on the held-out test split against the TRUE labels. Control: the same scores randomly re-assigned to rows.

Theoretical SD of AUC for label-independent scores on this split: 0.017.

Correlation between a permuted model's AUC and the Spearman correlation of its scores with the shipped model's scores: +0.977 (Pearson, n = 20). Rank correlation between |AUC - 0.5| and |rho|: +0.917.

Reading: the permuted-label models are centred on 0.5 (they learned no churn signal), but they spread far wider than independent scores, and their AUC moves with how much their scores happen to line up with the real model's. A model fitted to noise is still a function of tenure, charges and contract, which are strongly predictive, so by chance it can rank churners above or below chance. The demo's degraded candidate (0.64 after a Platt fit on permuted labels flipped its sign) is within this spread.
